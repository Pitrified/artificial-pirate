# Water far pattern

## The idea

The user's note, from the phone run of the fleet's giant ([`14_fleet`](../14_fleet/00_start.md)):
"in the chase camera (maybe now that it's more distant from the giant boat) we notice patterns in the waves.
the small ripples line up when looking far, and you see a faint bunch of lines or something".

## What the water draws now

From `Assets/Shaders/Water.shader`, `SO_WaterSettings.asset` and `Tools/asset_builders/textures.py`, read, not yet captured:

- Four Gerstner waves, 40, 24, 13 and 7 m long, from 20, 60, -15 and 100 degrees.
  In the fragment each fades as its wavelength nears four pixels, so far out only the long ones light the water.
- The detail normals: one map, `T_WaterNormal.png` (256 px), sampled three times on the world's xz.
  - Two near layers at a tiling of 0.12 and 0.204 a metre (a repeat every 8.3 m and 4.9 m), scrolling, both unrotated, at any distance.
  - A patch layer at a tenth of the tiling (83 m a repeat) at four times the strength, fading in from 60 m to 200 m from the camera.
- The map is a sum of 48 plane waves with integer frequencies 3 to 14 a tile, weighted by 1/f^1.2, so the few longest waves lead it.
  Seen whole it is streaks running one diagonal way (the image shows it plainly), not an even chop.
- The texture imports with bilinear filtering, mipmaps on, anisotropy 1 (off).

## Leads, in the order to test them

1. The streaks of the map, repeated with no rotation and the same orientation in all three layers.
   At a grazing angle every repeat lines up with the last, and the near layers do not fade with distance: rows of parallel lines out to the horizon.
   The patch layer, four times stronger, repeats the same streaks every 83 m: broad bands.
2. The mip choice without anisotropy: at a grazing angle the footprint is long one way, so the mip is picked by the long side and the short side blurs; the streaks perpendicular to the view survive longer than the rest, which favours lines across the view.
3. The four Gerstner waves: a periodic sum; far off the two long ones remain and their crests read as parallel lines.
   Real sea shows this too, so only an even, regular look is the fault.
4. The ripple square (48 m, around the ship): its edge fades, which should not show far off; checked to rule it out.

## Possible fixes, for after the probe

- A map without a lead direction: more waves with the falloff flattened, or a noise of another kind; regenerated by `textures.py`.
- Each layer rotated a different angle (a 2x2 matrix on the uv, cheap), so the repeats do not line up.
- The near layers fading with distance by the same pixel rule the waves use, so far out only what is resolved draws.
- Anisotropic filtering on the map (2x or 4x), judged by the phone's GPU time.
- The patch layer at another map or another rotation, or weaker.

## Open questions

- Q1: How to find which term draws the lines.
  It needs a capture from the chase camera behind the giant, then the same capture with each term off in turn.
  Each run is one PlayMode capture in batch mode, about 3 to 5 minutes, writing frames only in the session scratchpad; the toggles are temporary shader edits, reverted before any commit.
  - a. One capture first to reproduce it; then up to four runs, one per lead, before choosing a fix.
  - b. Skip the isolation, apply the likely fixes (a new map, rotated layers, the distance fade) and capture once.
  - Recommended: a, because the fix depends on which term it is, and five runs is about 25 minutes.
